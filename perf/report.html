Boms Graphics Performance

Most of the graphics cost is animation nobody can see. With a hand or table showing, stopping it cuts idle main-thread work by about 98% and idle GPU load by 80–89% on desktop and the emulated phone, with almost no visible change.

Measured on commit 756b252 · headless Edge 156 · Ryzen 5800X, RTX 3080 Ti · app code unchanged

Update: patches 01, 02, 07, 08, 09, 15 and 16 are now on main, along with a stricter version of 04. Patch 04 as implemented removes the foil only from table cards with at most 10% of their face showing, which is about 21–26 of 30 cards. The table below describes the original patch 04, so its memory figure does not apply to the implemented version.

Not measured: timings for the combined patches during play, the combined startup, how fast dialogs open, and the gain from the 10% rule. Another job has held the machine at near 100% CPU since 17:54, so timings from that period were thrown out and the queued run was cancelled. All other numbers here were measured before 17:54.

Effect on visual fidelity

The recommended set changes almost nothing you can see. On desktop no pixel moved by more than 4 levels out of 255 in any scene we could compare. On the phone, at most 0.06% of pixels changed visibly, on the table, because of patch 04.

What this check cannot tell you

  • Table clearing could not be verified. Cards scatter randomly, so the original differs from itself by 4.2% of pixels on desktop and 8.8% on the phone. Every patch fell in that same range.
  • Frames show position, not how motion feels. Patches 02, 04 and 16 change motion, so they are the three to judge by eye.
  • No person has compared these side by side yet, and the phone was emulated. Check patches 02 and 04 on a real phone before shipping.

Where the graphics cost comes from

  • Whether foil is on matters far more than which foil. An idle hand costs 6 ms/s of GPU without foil and 332–407 ms/s with any foil on desktop (7 and 242–313 on the phone). Foil raises render passes per frame from 4 to 42. Every foil costs about the same, so removing a particular one would not help.
  • Card styles are cheap. Drawing one card takes 0.16–0.55 ms. Neon is the most expensive and the only style with an SVG filter.
  • The "Simple shadows" setting helps less than expected. On the phone it cuts the 4-card freeze from 450 to 378 ms, while turning foil off reaches 266 ms.
  • What remains in the landing freeze: pose geometry, getAnimations and getBoundingClientRect. Patch 09 removes part of the first. On the phone the freeze is still about 350 ms with 30 cards on the table, the largest problem left during play.

Leads not yet chased

  • Selecting a card on the phone spends about 50 ms in the hand scroll step at game.js:944. Patch 01 did not change it, which ruled out my first guess. Writing scrollLeft forcing layout is the next suspect, unconfirmed.
  • Dragging settles into one of two states from run to run, with 115 or 121 layers on desktop and about 8 times the raster load in the larger one. Whichever triggers it costs real time, and the cause is unknown.
  • Startup on the phone blocks the main thread for about 1.3 s. Patch 15 reduces that to about 0.8 s, partly by shrinking a forced layout in neon-logo.js from 242 to 23 ms.

How far to trust this

  • The phone is an emulated 390×844 screen with the main thread slowed 4×, running on a desktop GPU. Treat its numbers as relative, and confirm on a real device.
  • The headless browser refreshes at 144 Hz, so per-second loads run higher than on a 60 Hz display. The GPU figure is busy time on the GPU process threads, a proxy for real GPU work.
  • Absolute phone figures drift between sessions (the same freeze measured 450 and 654 ms), so each comparison uses results measured together. Freezes use 5–7 runs.
  • The set was not measured on the HiDPI or software-GPU profiles, because those runs were lost to contamination.
  • The test suite had 17 failures on 756b252 before any patch, unrelated to this work, and patches are judged only by the failures they add.
